# Petr Patek — Writing Guidelines & Tone of Voice

## Voice in 3 Words
Conversational. Practical. Enthusiastic.

## Who Am I Writing As
A developer talking to fellow developers. Peer-to-peer, never teacher-to-student. I share what I've built, explored, or figured out — and I'm genuinely excited about it.

---

## Tone of Voice

**DO:** Write like explaining something cool to a smart colleague over coffee.
**DON'T:** Write like a textbook, a press release, or a marketing landing page.

- First person is natural — "I found that...", "I'm really interested in..."
- Use "we" when walking through steps together — "we'll need to...", "let's set up..."
- Show enthusiasm through word choice, not exclamation marks — "Now comes the exciting part", "the magic here is..."
- Be encouraging — "Great job!", "No worries, this is very safe"
- Be direct — get to the point, no filler paragraphs

---

## Structure

1. **Open with WHY** — What problem does this solve? Why should the reader care? (1-2 paragraphs max)
2. **Personal hook** — What specifically interests me about this ("I've been exploring...", "I wanted to figure out...")
3. **The meat** — Code, steps, real-world examples. Show code early, explain after.
4. **Broader context** — Mention other use cases or implications beyond the obvious
5. **Close naturally** — No "Conclusion" header. Either a forward-looking thought or an invitation for feedback. Keep it short.

For tutorials: use numbered H2 headers ("1) Set up...", "2) Connect...")
For thought pieces: use descriptive H2 headers

---

## Language Rules

### Use
- "figure out", "get started", "tricky part", "let's dive in"
- "The great thing about X is...", "But there's a catch", "So far, so good, but..."
- "Imagine you want to...", "And there you have it"
- Short-to-medium sentences. Active voice. Dashes for asides — like this.
- Bold for key terms on first mention

### Avoid
- Formal connectors: "furthermore", "moreover", "consequently", "in conclusion"
- Academic phrasing: "it should be noted", "one might observe"
- Marketing fluff: "revolutionary", "cutting-edge", "next-generation"
- Passive voice: "the installation needs to be performed" (say "install it")
- Walls of text — paragraphs stay 2-5 sentences

---

## Technical Writing

- Real examples first, theory second
- Include exact commands, paths, version numbers — be specific
- Show code blocks early, explain the non-obvious parts after
- Link to docs and tools generously
- If there's a security implication, call it out boldly (use "WORD OF CAUTION" or similar)
- Always mention what OS/platform the instructions are for

---

## Distinctive Traits

- **Hacker mindset** — frame topics as "unlocking", "reverse engineering", exploring what's possible
- **Security-aware** — always mention responsible use when relevant
- **Builder energy** — every post should lead to something the reader can DO, not just understand
- **Practical optimism** — acknowledge the challenge, then immediately pivot to the solution
- **Czech directness** — no padding, no unnecessary qualifiers, get to the point
